04 / Reflection

What changed during the term.

The biggest shift was learning to approach technology problems from more than a purely technical perspective.

My biggest challenge

At first, the number of business processes and stakeholder requirements involved in an enterprise application was difficult to navigate. Through meetings, documentation reviews, and collaboration with experienced team members, I became more comfortable translating business needs into design and technical recommendations.

Where I grew the most

I made the most progress in business analysis and user experience design. I became more confident gathering requirements, identifying user needs, and creating recommendations that balanced the user experience with business objectives.

Requirements UX Communication Critical thinking

A goal that evolved

I wanted deeper hands-on software development experience. Although I worked closely with developers and learned about development processes, my role focused more heavily on analysis, design, and requirements than implementation. That gave me a perspective on how decisions made before coding shape what developers ultimately build.

What surprised me

I was surprised by the scale and complexity of government systems and by how many stakeholders can be involved in relatively small changes. I also gained a greater appreciation for how accessibility, security, privacy, and governance influence decisions throughout the software development lifecycle.

05 / Looking Forward

What I am taking into my next role.

Good technology is not only about what can be built. It is about understanding what should be built, who it is for, and how it can work in the real world.

If I could repeat the term, I would spend more time early on learning the application's architecture and business processes. Having that context sooner would have allowed me to contribute more confidently to solution design and technical discussions.

I am leaving the work term with a stronger understanding of enterprise software projects, user-centered design, business analysis, and collaborative problem-solving. I also have a better understanding of how technical, business, and user requirements come together to create practical solutions.
